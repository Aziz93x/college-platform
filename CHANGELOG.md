### Step 15: the general framework

- The vice-dean, or whoever holds `framework.publish`, prepares the term's general framework on `/framework`: a draft of college-wide items that every department plan inherits. Migration `0016` adds `frameworks` and `framework_items`. Each item has:
  - a title, an axis and a kind (`taskKinds`, the registry Step 18 builds on);
  - whom it reaches: the holders of a role in each department, or each section's trainer;
  - when it is due: a week, every week, every few weeks, or after each day of the calendar of a kind;
  - what evidence it asks for: required or optional, the file types, how many, and whether the head approves it.
- Publishing makes a version that never changes; the next version starts as a draft copied from it. The publish sheet says how many tasks the version creates in how many departments, from one counting function, `countTasks()`, that the Step 20 engine reuses. Sections count from Step 17.
- Where the college asks for it (`framework.requireEndorsement`, off by default), the dean endorses the draft first, with the new key `framework.endorse`. A change to the draft takes the endorsement back. Since the dean now holds a key the vice-dean does not, the vice-dean's temporary passwords no longer reach the dean (ADR-0014's reach rule).
- Everyone reads the published framework of the term on view. The page is in everyone's navigation, so a trainer's phone now has a tab bar and Today leads a trainer to it.
- The term wizard copies the framework of the term it opens from into the new term as a draft.
- The log names `framework.create`, `frameworkItem.create`, `.update` and `.delete`, `framework.endorse` and `framework.publish` by the term.
- `seed:demo`: the vice-dean publishes a 10-item framework for the demo term.
- Tests: the counting function on fixtures; the versions, publication, endorsement and the wizard's copy on a real database; the members each department holds; the permission matrix and every action's audit event; the seed; and end-to-end on the desktop and the iPhone, the vice-dean starting, filling and publishing a framework with axe and 44 px targets, and a trainer reading the demo's.

### ADR-0018 amended: Founding Day, each year's holidays in their year, and approval (the owner's request)

- Founding Day joins the college's holidays for the ten years. It is 22 February in the Umm al-Qura year it falls in, moved off the weekend as the official rule says: a Friday gives the Thursday before, and a Saturday the Sunday after.
- The Eid al-Fitr break starts in the last third of Ramadan, so it is named «إجازة رمضان وعيد الفطر». There is no separate Ramadan holiday, as the owner chose. The demo term no longer adds Founding Day to its own calendar.
- `/terms` lists only the current Umm al-Qura year's holidays, under «إجازات عام 1448 هـ». Each year's holidays appear when that year begins, and every term still counts those that fall in it.
- The vice-dean approves an estimate as it is with «اعتماد», which writes `holiday.approve`, or adjusts its days in the sheet as before. Either way it then shows as approved.
- Tests:
  - Founding Day's estimate on a weekday, a Friday and a Saturday, and in a year without a 22 February.
  - The seed's holidays of 1448, and an approval, once, in the log.
  - End-to-end on the desktop and the iPhone: only this year's holidays are listed, and an estimate is approved, then adjusted.

### Step 13: terms and calendar

- The vice-dean, or whoever holds `term.manage`, enters the college's terms, and their dates follow from them. Migration `0014` adds `terms`, `calendar_events` and `holidays`, and a term on `memberships`.
  - `terms` holds the name as Rayat writes it, the first day, the number of weeks and the status (draft, active or closed). A name is unique in its college, and only one term is active.
  - `calendar_events` holds a term's holidays, exam weeks, grade deadlines and the college's own events. Each is one day or a range inside the term.
  - `holidays` holds the college's Eid breaks (ADR-0018, below).
- Weeks are counted from the term's first day, seven days each (`src/modules/terms/domain/weeks.ts`, pure and unit-tested).
  - A week whose working days are all holidays is marked as a holiday. While `calendar.skipHolidayWeeks` is on (the default), it gets no number and the term runs a week longer.
  - The working days are the first `calendar.workingDays` days (5) from `locale.weekStart`.
  - `weekOf()` gives the week a day falls in, and `termEndsOn()` the term's last day.
  - Settings has a new calendar group with these two settings and `calendar.termWeeks`, 18, the new term form's default.
- `/terms` lists the college's terms, newest first.
  - A new term takes a name, a first day from the date picker (now with a one-day mode) and its weeks on the stepper.
  - A term's page shows its status, its calendar and its weeks, with this week marked, and a history panel for managers. The calendar is edited in a sheet: kind, title and days. Anyone signed in may open an active or closed term's page; only managers see drafts and the controls.
  - Activating a term closes the active one in the same transaction. A closed term no longer changes. A change that would leave a calendar event outside its term is refused.
  - "فتح الفصل التالي" makes the next term and copies the calendar, except the holidays, to the same places in its weeks. Steps 15, 18 and 19 add their copies to it.
  - The log names `term.create`, `term.update`, `term.activate`, `term.close` and `calendarEvent.create`, `.update` and `.delete` by the term's name, with their days and kinds in words.
- The header's term switcher, BeUI's morph popover used as a select, sits before the bell. It shows the term on view with today's week, and lists the terms the viewer may see (drafts only for managers) with a link to the term's calendar.
  - The choice is kept in a cookie, and `viewedTerm()` (`src/app/(app)/_shell/term.ts`) reads it for every page.
  - Today says "الأسبوع 9 من 18", a holiday week, or the day the term begins.
  - On a phone, Today now loads 310,322 bytes of script, up from 279,435 after Step 12. That covers both the announcements' dialog and the switcher.
- A role may be given for one term from the role's page. Such a membership grants its keys only while its term is active. A system role is given for every term, and a closed term is not offered.
- `seed:demo --week N` (default 9, from 1 to 18) makes an 18-week active term in which today falls in week N, counted past the Eid breaks. `pnpm local:up --demo --week N` passes it on.
  - The vice-dean enters the term's calendar: a meeting to open the term in week 1, the midterm exams in week 8, an extra holiday in week 11, the final exams in weeks 17 and 18, the grade deadline on the last working day, and National Day or Founding Day where they fall in the term.
  - One trainer of Electrical is the department's secretary for this term only.
- Tests:
  - Unit tests for the weeks across Ramadan and the Eid breaks, on the dates of the second term of 1447, and for the break estimates.
  - Integration tests for the services: each refusal, activating and closing, the calendar, the next term's copy, and a term counting a break and following its adjustment. The seed's term in week 9 and with `--week 3`.
  - Every action's audit event and its rows in the permission matrix.
  - End-to-end on the desktop and the iPhone:
    - The vice-dean makes an 18-week term with a week of holiday and an exam week, then opens the next term from it.
    - The vice-dean adjusts an Eid break.
    - A trainer sees the term and its week in the header and on Today, opens the calendar from the switcher, and is refused `/terms`.
    - axe and 44 px targets throughout.

### ADR-0018: the Eid breaks, ten years ahead (the owner's request)

- The base seed adds the college's Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha breaks for the current Hijri year and the nine after it. It adds nothing that is already there.
- Each break is estimated from past Saudi calendars (docs/reviews/eid-breaks.md) and fitted to whole weeks.
  - Eid al-Fitr runs from 12 days before Eid to 5 after it, which covers the last third of Ramadan. Eid al-Adha runs from 7 days before to 6 after.
  - Each Eid's date comes from the Umm al-Qura calendar that Intl carries, so nothing is fetched.
  - Checked against the announced breaks, the estimate is exact in most years and at most two days off.
- `/terms` lists the breaks as estimated or adjusted. The vice-dean sets each one to its announced days in a sheet, which writes `holiday.update`. A break that has begun no longer changes.
- Every term counts the breaks that fall in it with its own holidays. One adjustment moves the weeks of every term it touches, and the term's page lists the breaks with its calendar, marked as the college's.

### ADR-0014: two-factor is the college's choice

- Two-factor is optional, as the owner asked. A new security setting, "Require two-factor for admin keys" (`auth.requireTwoFactor`, off by default, confirmed with the password), decides whether holding a key that requires two-factor sends its holder to set it up before anything else, as ADR-0009 did for every admin; accounts that have two-factor keep it either way.
- Anyone turns two-factor on from their profile, through the setup page, which brings them back to the profile, and off again there with their password typed again (it counts toward the lockout, as at sign-in). While the setting holds them to it, the profile says so and offers no way to turn it off. Turning it off removes the app's secret and the backup codes.
- An admin turns off someone's two-factor from their page or row, after a lost phone, with the temporary password's key and reach, so a head helps their own trainers only. Both ways write `account.disableTwoFactor`, with the person or the admin as actor; the person page's account sheet confirms it, and the history shows it.
- The setup's last step sends the person on itself: to Today when the college required it, back to the profile when they chose it. Turning two-factor on gives the session a new cookie, and the setup page drawn again in the action's answer used to send the person to sign in and on through `/`.
- `seed:demo`'s history has a head who lost their phone: the admin turns off their two-factor, and they set it up again on the new one.
- Tests: unit tests for the viewer with the setting on and off, the profile action's refusal while required, the setup's destinations, the person view's new action and both actions in the permission matrix; integration tests for turning it off as an admin (the secret gone, the password alone signing in) and as its owner (the lockout counting), and both actions' events; end-to-end tests where a trainer turns two-factor on from the profile and off with the password, an admin without it is held to the setup while the setting is on and cannot turn it off, and an admin turns off a trainer's two-factor, who then signs in with the password alone. The stack's first-run test reaches Today after the password change and sets two-factor up from the profile.

### Audit 1: steps 1-3

- `check:hardcode` catches role-name checks written on one line. On a line that mentions `role`, it flags a role-like string literal (one with no dot or slash, so permission keys such as `"roles.assign"` and paths pass) that is compared, searched for with `.includes`, `.has`, `.startsWith` or `.endsWith`, passed to `hasRole()` or a Drizzle comparison (`eq`, `ne`, `inArray`, `notInArray`, `like`, `ilike`), listed in an assigned array or compared in SQL (`= 'x'`); and an array or `Set` literal searched with `.includes(` or `.has(`. It also flags a `case "x"` in the innermost `switch` on a role, a `SCREAMING_CASE` name with a `ROLE` or `ROLES` part, and an `enum` named after roles. docs/reviews/audit-03.md lists the forms it misses.
- `check:boundaries` makes `domain/` an allowlist: it imports only its own module's `domain/` files, `zod`, `vitest` and types from a module's `index.ts`, so npm I/O packages such as `pg`, `pg-boss`, `drizzle-orm` and `next/headers` fail too, in tests as well.
- `audit_events` is append-only in the database: migration `0001_audit_events_append_only.sql` adds triggers that reject `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE`. They stop mistakes, not the table owner, which is the app's own database user.
- CI runs the Playwright smoke test against the standalone production build (`node .next/standalone/server.js`) instead of the dev server.
- ADR-0004 (proposed): the repository stays public, and licensed or non-public brand assets never enter git. `.gitignore` ignores `*.woff`, `*.woff2`, `*.ttf`, `*.otf` and `public/brand/`, a unit test fails when a font or brand file is tracked, and docs/PLAN.md Step 4 carries the rule.

### Step 03: database, jobs and platform core

- Drizzle ORM on PostgreSQL through `pg`, with a lazy pool so `next build` needs no database. Each module owns its tables: `colleges` (org), `settings` (settings) and `audit_events` (audit), in the first migration `db/migrations/0000_platform_core.sql`.

Every setting is college-scoped for now.
- `worker/index.ts` runs pg-boss: a `heartbeat` job scheduled every minute, and a graceful stop on SIGTERM or SIGINT that waits up to 8 s for running jobs, then exits 0, or exits 1 when the stop fails or is still running at 9.5 s, before Docker's SIGKILL. It logs `worker started` only once those signal handlers are installed. A failed start is logged as JSON and exits 1.
- pino JSON logs on stdout; `src/proxy.ts` gives every request a new `x-request-id`, replacing any the client sent, and returns it on the response. Server code logs through `getRequestLogger()`, which adds it to each line, and `onRequestError` in `src/instrumentation.ts` logs every server error Next reports with it. Worker lines carry the queue and job id instead. `/healthz` reports the process alive; `/readyz` checks the database and the queue and answers 503 when either fails.
- `check:boundaries` also checks `worker/` and `db/`, which use a module only through its `index.ts`.
